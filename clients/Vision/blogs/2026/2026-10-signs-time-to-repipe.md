# Signs It's Time to Repipe Your Kelowna Home

> **Quick Answer:** The most common signs you need repiping are leaks that keep showing up in different spots, pinhole leaks in copper pipe, rusty or discoloured water, low water pressure throughout the house, and visible corrosion on pipes and fittings. Homes with ageing galvanized steel, Poly-B or Kitec piping are the most likely candidates. One leak can usually be repaired, but repeat failures across the system usually mean a whole house repipe is the better long-term fix, and an in-home assessment is the way to confirm it.

Most homeowners never think about the pipes inside their walls until one of them leaks, and a single leak is rarely the whole story. If your home in Kelowna, BC has decades-old pipes, or a material the industry has since moved away from, the first leak often means the rest of the system is wearing out at the same rate. This guide covers the signs you need repiping, how to tell a one-off repair from a system-wide problem, and what a repipe involves, whatever your pipes are made of.

## Why Pipe Material and Age Matter

Every water supply pipe has a working life, and the material largely decides how it fails. Kelowna and West Kelowna have homes from nearly every building era, so local plumbers see the full range:

- **Galvanized steel:** steel pipe coated in zinc, common in older homes. As the coating wears away, the steel rusts from the inside, which narrows the pipe and eventually leads to leaks at threaded joints.
- **Copper:** long-lasting and still used today, but older copper can develop pinhole leaks, especially where water chemistry or high flow wears the pipe wall thin over time.
- **Poly-B:** the grey plastic pipe found in many homes built from the late 1970s to the mid-1990s. Our page on [Poly-B plumbing replacement in Kelowna](https://visionplumbingandheating.com/residential/poly-b-plumbing-kelowna/) covers why insurers treat it as high risk.
- **Kitec:** a plastic and aluminium pipe with brass fittings, common in homes built between about 1995 and 2007. See our [Kitec plumbing issues and replacement](https://visionplumbingandheating.com/kelowna-kitec-plumbing/) page for how to identify it.

Older homes may also have fittings and solder that no longer meet today's standards. Health Canada notes that [plumbing codes allowed lead solder until 1986 and that older brass fittings may contain lead](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/environmental-workplace-health/reports-publications/water-quality/what-about-lead.html), so the age of the joints matters as much as the pipe itself.

## Signs You Can See, Hear and Feel

The clearest signs you need repiping show up in day-to-day use. Two or more together usually point to the whole system rather than a single bad section.

### Leaks That Keep Coming Back

One leak under a sink is a repair. A leak in the basement ceiling in spring, another behind the laundry room wall in summer and a third near the water heater by fall is a pattern: the pipe is wearing out everywhere, and each repair only fixes the spot that failed first. Damp drywall, bubbling paint and musty smells can all point to hidden leaks. If you suspect water is escaping somewhere you can't see, [professional water leak detection](https://visionplumbingandheating.com/water-leak-detection-kelowna/) can find it without opening walls at random.

### Pinhole Leaks in Copper Pipe

Pinhole leaks in copper pipes start as tiny holes that spray or drip, often leaving a green or blue-green stain. Pinholes form when the pipe wall has thinned from the inside, so by the time one appears, nearby sections have usually thinned too. A plumber can patch a pinhole, but if your copper lines have already needed several patches, it's worth asking whether replacing the affected runs, or the whole system, makes more sense.

### Rusty or Discoloured Water

Brown, orange or yellow water, especially first thing in the morning, is a classic sign of corroded pipes, most often galvanized steel. If the colour shows up only on the hot side, the [hot water tank](https://visionplumbingandheating.com/residential/plumbing/hot-water-tanks-kelowna/) may be the source instead. Discolouration on every tap that doesn't clear after running the water points toward the supply piping.

### Low Water Pressure Throughout the House

Weak pressure at one fixture is often a clogged aerator or a failing valve. Weak pressure everywhere, particularly when two fixtures run at once, can mean rust or mineral scale has narrowed the inside of the pipes. Our post on [how hard water affects plumbing in Kelowna](https://visionplumbingandheating.com/how-hard-water-affects-plumbing-in-kelowna/) explains how scale builds up over time. Our [home water pressure guide](https://visionplumbingandheating.com/understanding-home-water-pressure-guide/) covers the other causes worth ruling out first.

## Signs That Don't Show Up at the Tap

Some of the strongest reasons to repipe have nothing to do with a leak you can see.

- **Your home has a known problem material.** If an inspection or a home purchase report has confirmed Poly-B or Kitec, replacement is usually a question of when, not whether. Our comparison of [Poly-B piping vs. Kitec piping](https://visionplumbingandheating.com/poly-b-piping-or-kitec-piping/) helps you tell them apart.
- **Your insurer is asking questions.** Some insurers ask about pipe material at renewal, and a problem material can affect your coverage or premium. Your broker can tell you what documentation they need.
- **You're planning a renovation.** If walls and ceilings are already open for a kitchen, bathroom or basement project, replacing old supply lines at the same time is far less disruptive than opening them again later.

## Repair or Repipe: How to Tell the Difference

A repair makes sense when the problem is isolated, and a repipe makes sense when the problem is the system. The difference comes down to how many failures you've had, where they are and what the pipe looks like inside.

A single leak from a damaged section, a loose fitting or a nail through a pipe is a repair. Repeat leaks in different locations, corrosion wherever a technician looks, or a material your insurer won't accept point toward repiping a house rather than patching it. A partial repipe is also an option in some homes, such as replacing the remaining galvanized sections when part of the house has already been updated.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I know if my house needs to be repiped?

The strongest signs are repeated leaks in different locations, pinhole leaks in copper, rusty or discoloured water on both hot and cold taps, and low water pressure throughout the home. A home with Poly-B, Kitec or ageing galvanized steel is also a likely candidate even before problems start. A plumber can confirm the pipe material and condition during an inspection.

### Can you repipe only part of a house?

Yes. A partial repipe replaces only the sections that are failing or made of a problem material, which is common when part of a home has already been updated during a renovation. If the same material runs through the whole house and is failing in several places, a full repipe is usually more practical than replacing it in stages.

### Does repiping include the water line from the street?

Usually not. A repipe covers the supply lines inside the home, while the service line that brings water from the street to the house is a separate job. If that line is old or leaking, it is assessed and replaced on its own, sometimes using trenchless methods that avoid digging a long trench.

### Will home insurance cover damage from old pipes?

Home insurance in Canada is generally designed to cover sudden and accidental water damage from indoor plumbing. Damage from slow, long-term leaks or from piping an insurer has already flagged may be treated differently, and policies vary. Ask your insurer or broker how your pipe material affects your coverage.
