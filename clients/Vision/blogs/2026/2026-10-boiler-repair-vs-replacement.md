# Boiler Repair vs. Replacement: How to Decide

> **Quick Answer:** When weighing boiler repair vs replacement, start with three questions: how old is the boiler, how much is this repair compared with a new unit, and is there any safety concern? A younger boiler with a single failed part is usually worth repairing. An older boiler that needs frequent or expensive repairs, has a leaking or corroded heat exchanger, or raises any carbon monoxide concern is usually a candidate for replacement. An in-home assessment by a licensed gas technician is the only way to know for certain.

If your boiler has started acting up as the nights get colder, the next question is usually the same: fix it or replace it? Boiler repair vs replacement is rarely a simple yes or no, because the boiler is only one part of a hydronic system of pumps, valves, piping and radiators or in-floor loops. In Kelowna and West Kelowna, where boilers run hard from October through March, a wrong call can mean paying for a repair on a unit that fails again in January, or replacing a boiler that had years left in it. This guide walks through the five things our technicians weigh on every call: age, symptoms, repair cost, efficiency and safety. If you already know you need a new system, our separate guide to boiler installation costs covers pricing.

## Start With the Boiler's Age

Age is the first filter, because it tells you how much life a repair is likely to buy. Most residential boilers we service in the Central Okanagan last [VISION TO CONFIRM: typical boiler lifespan range Vision sees, e.g. 15 to 20 years for wall-hung condensing units and longer for cast-iron units] with regular maintenance. You can usually find the manufacture date on the rating plate inside the cabinet.

- **Under about 10 years old:** repair is almost always the better call unless there is a safety issue. Parts are easy to find, and the boiler is likely close to current efficiency standards.
- **In the middle of its expected life:** decide case by case. One repair is reasonable, but a second major repair within a season or two is a sign to start planning ahead.
- **At or past its expected life:** replacement deserves a serious look, even for a modest repair. Planning a replacement in the fall is far easier than an emergency swap during a cold snap.

## Symptoms That Usually Mean a Repair

Many boiler problems come down to one worn component. Our [boiler repair and installation services](https://visionplumbingandheating.com/kelowna-boiler-services/) handle these repairs regularly.

- **No heat with a fault code:** a failed igniter, flame sensor or control board is a common cause. These are individual parts, and replacing one is usually a straightforward repair.
- **Pressure that drops or climbs:** this often points to a worn pressure relief valve, a waterlogged expansion tank or a small leak in the piping. Each can be corrected without touching the boiler itself.
- **Some rooms or zones cold, others fine:** a stuck zone valve, a failing circulator pump or air in the lines is the usual culprit, not the boiler itself.
- **Gurgling and a shutdown during a cold snap:** on condensing boilers, a frozen condensate line is a frequent cause, which our [boiler maintenance in winter](https://visionplumbingandheating.com/boiler-maintenance-in-winter/) post covers in more detail.

## Signs It May Be Time for a Replacement

Some problems point past individual parts to the boiler itself.

- **A leaking or corroded heat exchanger:** the heat exchanger is the core of the boiler. Once it is leaking, corroded through or cracked, the repair cost on most residential units approaches the cost of a new boiler.
- **Repeat breakdowns:** several service calls a season add up quickly, and they usually mean more parts are wearing out at once.
- **Parts that are hard to get:** older or discontinued models can wait days or weeks for components, which is a real problem in a Kelowna January.
- **Rising gas bills with no change in use:** a boiler that is losing efficiency burns more fuel to keep the house at the same temperature.

## The Repair Cost Rule of Thumb

A simple comparison helps when the decision is close. Many heating contractors use the 50% rule: if a single repair would cost about half of what a new boiler would, replacement is usually the better use of that money, especially on an older unit.

A second check combines age and price. Multiply the boiler's age by the repair quote, and if the result is higher than the cost of a replacement, the repair is unlikely to pay for itself before the next major failure. Neither rule replaces a technician's judgment, but both stop you from sinking money into a unit near the end of its life. To use them, you need a written repair quote and a realistic replacement figure for your home; our boiler installation cost guide explains what drives replacement pricing in the Kelowna area.

## What an Older Boiler Costs You in Efficiency

An older boiler costs you money every month, even when it is working. Natural Resources Canada requires new household gas hot water boilers manufactured on or after July 1, 2023 to meet a [minimum AFUE of 90%](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/energy-efficiency/industry-energy-efficiency/energy-efficiency-regulations-standards-industry/gas-boilers-household), which means any new gas boiler you install today is a high-efficiency condensing model. Boilers from the 1970s, 1980s and 1990s were built to much lower standards, so the gap on your gas bill can be significant.

There is a catch. A condensing boiler only reaches its rated efficiency when the water returning to it is cool enough for the exhaust gases to condense. Older radiator systems were often designed to run hot, so a new boiler on an old system may underperform unless the controls are set up for it, while in-floor radiant heating is a natural match. If you are considering an upgrade, a technician experienced in [hydronic heating installation](https://visionplumbingandheating.com/residential/plumbing/hydronic-heating-installation-kelowna/) should look at the whole system, not just the boiler.

## Safety Always Comes First

Safety concerns override cost. A gas boiler that is not burning or venting properly can produce carbon monoxide, and Technical Safety BC lists boilers among the common household [sources of carbon monoxide](https://www.technicalsafetybc.ca/carbon-monoxide). It recommends annual inspections by a licensed gas contractor and Canadian-certified CO alarms on every floor and near bedrooms.

Warning signs include soot around the boiler or vent, a yellow burner flame instead of a crisp blue one, or a separated or corroded vent pipe. If a CO alarm sounds, get everyone outside and call 911 before calling a contractor. Our post on [carbon monoxide safety checks](https://visionplumbingandheating.com/carbon-monoxide-safety-checks/) explains what a technician inspects. A boiler with a cracked heat exchanger or a venting problem that cannot be safely corrected should be replaced, whatever its age.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does a boiler last?

Most residential boilers last [VISION TO CONFIRM: same lifespan range as the body, e.g. 15 to 20 years for wall-hung condensing units and longer for cast-iron units] with annual maintenance. Water quality, how hard the boiler works each winter and how well it was sized and installed all affect the real number, and a boiler serviced every year will generally outlast one that is only looked at when it breaks.

### When should I replace my boiler instead of repairing it?

Replacement usually makes sense when the boiler is near or past its expected lifespan, needs repeated repairs, has a leaking or cracked heat exchanger, or has a safety issue that cannot be corrected. A common rule of thumb is to replace when a single repair would cost about half the price of a new boiler. Rising gas bills and hard-to-find parts are further signs that a unit is near the end of its useful life.

### Is a new boiler more efficient than an old one?

Yes. In Canada, household gas hot water boilers manufactured on or after July 1, 2023 must meet a minimum AFUE of 90%, while many boilers from the 1970s to 1990s were built to far lower standards. A condensing boiler only reaches that rating when return water is cool enough for the exhaust to condense, so the distribution system matters too.

### Can a boiler cause carbon monoxide poisoning?

Yes. A gas boiler that is not burning fuel completely or venting properly can release carbon monoxide into the home. Technical Safety BC recommends annual inspections of gas appliances by a licensed gas contractor and Canadian-certified CO alarms on every floor and near bedrooms, replaced every 7 to 10 years.
