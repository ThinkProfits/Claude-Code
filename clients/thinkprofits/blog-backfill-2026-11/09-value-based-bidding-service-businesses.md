# Value-Based Bidding for Service Businesses: When It Makes Sense

[IMAGE: ThinkProfits branded blog placeholder | alt: Value-based bidding for service businesses in Google Ads | file: /images/blog/wp/tp-branded-placeholder.jpg]

> **Quick answer:** Value-based bidding tells Google Ads to chase the leads that are worth more, not just more leads. It fits a service business when you can send back at least two different, honest values per lead, ideally from your CRM, and have enough conversions for Smart Bidding to learn from. If every lead is worth the same to you, or you close a handful of jobs a month, Target CPA on qualified leads is usually the safer setting.

Most service businesses run Google Ads on conversion counts. A form fill is a form fill, and the bidding system treats a $400 repair enquiry the same as a $40,000 renovation request. Value-based bidding changes that by giving each conversion a dollar value, so Smart Bidding can lean toward the searches that tend to produce bigger or more likely jobs.

For some accounts that is a genuine upgrade. For others it trains the bidding system on numbers nobody in the business would sign off on. This guide covers what changes when you switch, what Google's own documentation asks for first, how to set lead values without inventing them, and the situations where we leave a Vancouver service business on count-based bidding.

## What changes when you bid on value

The question Google is answering moves from "how many conversions can I get?" to "how much conversion value can I get?" In Google Ads, that means one of two strategies.

- **Maximize Conversion Value:** Google spends your daily budget trying to collect as much total conversion value as possible, with no efficiency target. It suits accounts that want growth within a fixed budget.
- **Target ROAS:** Google still chases value, but aims for the return on ad spend you set, such as $5 of conversion value per $1 spent. Google notes that from June 2026 the interface labels this simply "Target ROAS," with no change to how it bids.

The count-based counterparts are Maximize Conversions and Target CPA. If you have read our guide on [setting a Target CPA from real business numbers](/digital-news/target-cpa-vs-business-cpa-google-ads/), this is the next step up: instead of one acceptable cost per lead, you tell Google which leads deserve a higher bid. The catch is that Smart Bidding only knows what you tell it, so guessed values produce confidently wrong bids.

## What Google asks for before you switch

Google publishes fairly specific prerequisites in its help page on [Smart Bidding with value-based bidding for Search](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15099424).

| Requirement | What Google's documentation says | What it means for a service business |
|---|---|---|
| Distinct values | Report two or more different values to your conversion goals | One flat value on every lead gives the system nothing to choose between |
| Conversion volume | At least 15 conversions in the last 30 days for Target ROAS | Many small trades and clinics sit below this on qualified leads alone |
| Short conversion delay | Ideally under 7 days, sent as soon as they happen | A "job won" signal that arrives six weeks later is too slow to bid on |
| One funnel stage | Optimize for one stage of the lead-to-sale journey | Do not mix raw form fills and closed jobs in the same goal |
| Ramp-up data | Upload values for 3 weeks or 1 to 2 conversion cycles, whichever is longer, before switching | Collect values first, switch second |

Google's separate best practices page also advises against zero-value conversions and recommends daily uploads where possible.

## Where lead values come from

The honest answer is your sales records, not a spreadsheet of hopes. There are three practical ways to give leads a value, from simplest to most accurate.

1. **Static values by lead type.** Each conversion action gets a fixed value based on what it is historically worth. If booked estimates close more often than general contact forms, the estimate request gets the higher value. This needs a CRM that can show close rate and average job value by lead source over a few months.
2. **Values from form answers.** If your form asks for the service or project size, those answers can set the value at the moment of conversion. Google lists lead form answers as one way to separate higher-quality leads. The risk is that people pick the biggest option to get a faster callback, so check answers against what actually closed.
3. **Offline values from your CRM.** The click is tracked, then your CRM sends back a qualified or won status with a real value. Google recommends [enhanced conversions for leads](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13489421) over the older click-ID import for this, with daily uploads. It is the most accurate route and the one with the most setup.

To build a proxy value, work backwards: average contribution per won job, multiplied by the share of that lead type that becomes a won job. If a won job contributes $3,000 and one in ten estimate requests becomes a job, each estimate request is worth roughly $300 to you. Those figures are an illustration, not a benchmark, so use your own and review them quarterly.

Spam and junk leads should not be sent as conversions at all. Google's value-based bidding best practices advise against zero-value conversions, so filter junk out rather than giving it a value of $0. Our [lead quality feedback loop](/digital-news/google-ads-lead-quality-loop/) covers how to mark these leads in the CRM so they stop teaching the bidding system to find more of them.

## Does value-based bidding fit your business?

The right answer depends on how different your leads really are and whether you can prove it with data.

### When it makes sense

Bidding on value earns its keep when the spread between your best and worst leads is wide and you can measure it. The strongest candidates we see share a few traits.

- **Big gaps in job value.** A renovation company doing both $2,000 repairs and $80,000 additions has a real reason to bid differently on each. A business where most jobs land in a narrow band gains little from the extra complexity.
- **A CRM that records outcomes.** If your team marks leads as qualified, quoted and won with a dollar amount, the raw material already exists.
- **Enough volume on the stage you optimize.** Fifteen conversions a month is Google's floor for Target ROAS, not a comfortable level. Accounts well above it tend to settle faster.
- **Budget that is not the constraint.** Google recommends unconstrained budgets with ROAS targets. If your campaigns are [limited by budget rather than demand](/digital-news/budget-limited-vs-demand-limited-google-ads/), the system has less room to pick the higher-value auctions.

### When to stay on Target CPA instead

Google's lead generation guidance says that if lead quality matters but you cannot assign distinct values, Target CPA on qualified or converted leads is the recommended route. That describes plenty of small Canadian service businesses, and it is a perfectly respectable place to be.

Stay on count-based bidding for now if you close fewer than 15 jobs or qualified leads a month and cannot move the goal earlier without losing meaning. The same applies if your "values" would be one number on every lead, or your sales cycle runs several weeks with no reliable earlier stage. And if your tracking still counts duplicate form fills or page views as primary conversions, fix that first with our [primary vs secondary conversions checklist](/digital-news/primary-vs-secondary-conversions-google-ads/), because bidding on value multiplies bad data rather than correcting it.

Reporting matters too. After the switch, conversion value and ROAS are only as believable as the CRM behind them, so your [marketing reporting](/digital-marketing-reporting/) needs to tie value back to won jobs.

## How we roll it out without wrecking a working account

We test the switch on part of the account before anything else moves. This is the sequence we use.

1. **Pick the funnel stage.** We choose the single stage the values will attach to (usually qualified lead or booked estimate) and confirm the conversion setup is already clean before any values flow.
2. **Send values while still on Target CPA.** Values flow for at least Google's ramp-up period, so the system has history before it acts.
3. **Test on one campaign.** We move a single campaign with steady volume and leave the rest as a comparison. A Google Ads experiment is the cleaner option when volume allows a split.
4. **Set the first ROAS target from history.** Google advises basing it on your recent actual ROAS, not an aspirational number. A target set too high starves the campaign of traffic.
5. **Judge on won-job value, not platform ROAS.** After a few conversion cycles we compare CRM revenue per dollar spent against the control. Every change goes into our [weekly Google Ads change log](/digital-news/weekly-google-ads-change-log/) so cause and effect stay traceable.

Results vary by market, season and data quality, so we do not promise a lift. A careful rollout gives you a clear answer on whether the new strategy beats your current one in your account.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can value-based bidding work for lead generation without online sales?

Yes. Google allows proxy values such as a lead score or an estimated value per lead type, so you do not need online revenue. The values need to reflect real differences in what leads are worth, which usually means building them from CRM close rates and average job value.

### How many conversions do I need for Target ROAS?

Google Ads Help lists at least 15 conversions in the last 30 days for Target ROAS on Search. If you only reach it by counting every raw form fill, Target CPA on qualified leads may serve you better until volume grows.

### What is the difference between Maximize Conversion Value and Maximize Conversions?

Maximize Conversions tries to get the most conversions for your budget, treating each one equally. Maximize Conversion Value tries to get the highest total value, so it will accept fewer leads if they are worth more. The second only helps if your values are accurate and different from each other.

### Should I import offline conversions first?

For most service businesses, yes. Offline data is the most reliable way to send real job values back, and Google recommends enhanced conversions for leads with daily uploads. Start uploading while still on your current strategy so the account has weeks of value history before you switch.

### How long before we know if it is working?

Plan for the ramp-up period plus a few conversion cycles, which for most service accounts means one to three months. Judging after a week mostly measures the learning period, not the strategy.
