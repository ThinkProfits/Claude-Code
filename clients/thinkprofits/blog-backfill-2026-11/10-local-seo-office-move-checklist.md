# Local SEO Office Move: How to Change Your Business Address on Google in 30 Days

> **Quick answer:** A local SEO office move is mostly a sequencing job. To change your business address on Google, edit the address on your existing Google Business Profile (never create a new one), verify again when Google asks, then update your website, citations and ad accounts so every source shows the same new address. Plan the work across roughly 30 days: two weeks of preparation, the move itself, and two weeks of cleanup and monitoring.

The lease is signed and the movers are booked. What usually gets left until the last minute is the online version of the move: the address Google, your website and dozens of directories show to people looking for you.

A local SEO office move is less about rankings tricks and more about sequencing. Change things in the wrong order and you can end up with a profile stuck in verification, a duplicate listing at the old address, or ads sending people to an empty unit in Burnaby. Below is the 30-day checklist we use for Metro Vancouver relocations, covering your Google Business Profile, website, citations, redirects and Google Ads, plus an honest note on what to expect afterwards.

## Days 1 to 14: build a move record before you touch anything

The first two weeks are for writing down what you have, so nothing gets missed once the boxes start moving.

Start a single "move record" spreadsheet with three columns: the old details, the new details, and where each one lives. The new details should be written exactly the way you want them to appear everywhere, including the unit number format ("Suite 210" or "#210", pick one) and the postal code. This is the same canonical fact record we recommend in our guide to [local business entity consistency](/digital-news/local-business-entity-consistency-ai-search/). It also settles any later debate about which version is correct.

Then inventory every place your address appears:

- **Your Google Business Profile and any Apple, Bing or other map listings.** Note who has owner or manager access to each one, because only those people can make the edit.
- **Your website.** Check the footer, contact page, location pages, embedded maps and structured data (schema), since a forgotten footer is one of the most common leftovers we find after a move.
- **Directories and citations.** List the directories and data aggregators that carry your details, with the login for each, so cleanup week is not spent on password resets.
- **Paid and offline channels.** Include Google Ads, Meta pages, email signatures and invoices, since a wrong address on an invoice will not hurt rankings but does confuse customers.

Before the move, take a baseline. Record your current local rankings for your main service terms, ideally with a [geo-grid report](/digital-news/local-seo-geo-grid-report-vancouver/), plus your Business Profile calls, direction requests and website clicks. Without a "before" picture you cannot tell a move effect from a slow month.

## Day 15: change your business address on Google without creating a duplicate

On the day you start operating from the new address, update the address on your existing Business Profile. Google is explicit about this: its guidance on [duplicate profiles and relocations](https://support.google.com/business/answer/12756178) says that if your business relocates, you should not create a new profile for the new location but update the existing one instead.

In your Business Profile, go to **Edit profile**, then **Location**, and edit the business location. Enter the complete street address, including suite or unit numbers, and adjust the map pin if it lands on the wrong building. Google says edits usually appear within about 10 minutes but can take up to 30 days.

Three things to know before you press save:

- **You will probably need to verify again.** Google's help documentation states that if you move a verified business to a new address, you must verify it again. Google chooses the method (video, phone or text, email, live video call or postcard), so have someone at the new office who can show signage and the space if asked.
- **Do not keep editing during a postcard verification.** If Google mails a postcard code, it warns against changing your name, address or category, or requesting a new code, while you wait, because that can make the mailed code stop working.
- **Your reviews should follow you.** Google's review help says that if you move and keep the same business name, reviews are transferred automatically, with exceptions for certain categories such as hotels, golf courses and scenic attractions. Separate a rebrand from a move where you can.

If you are a service-area business that hides its address, the same rules apply: edit the existing profile and keep the address hidden if you do not meet customers there.

## Days 15 to 21: update your website, schema and location pages

Once the profile edit is in, your website should match it exactly. It is the one source you fully control.

1. **Update every visible address.** Change the footer, contact page, about page and any location pages to the exact wording in your move record. Search your site for the old street name and postal code to catch stray mentions in PDFs and old posts.
2. **Update structured data.** If your site uses LocalBusiness schema, change the address and any geo coordinates in the code. Google lists `address` as a required property for local business structured data, so an old address there is a genuine error rather than a cosmetic one.
3. **Replace embedded maps.** Swap any Google Maps embed so it points at the new location, then test it on a phone as well as a desktop.
4. **Handle location page URLs carefully.** If a page URL contained the old neighbourhood (for example `/burnaby-office/`), set a permanent 301 redirect to the new page rather than deleting it. Our [sitemap hygiene checklist](/digital-news/sitemap-hygiene-website-rebuild/) covers how to check redirects and update your XML sitemap afterwards.

## Days 15 to 28: clean up citations and directories

Citations are mentions of your business name, address and phone number on other sites. Keeping them consistent (often called NAP consistency) means customers, and the platforms that copy your details from each other, see one version of the truth.

Prioritize by impact:

- **Major maps and platforms first.** Update your Apple Maps and Bing Places listings, plus any social profile that shows an address, since customers use these for directions too.
- **Industry and local directories next.** Professional and local business associations and niche directories can rank for your brand name, so an old address there can send a customer to the wrong door.
- **Data aggregators and long-tail listings last.** These change slowly, so log each submission date in your move record and check back rather than resubmitting.

If a duplicate Google listing appears at the old address, use Google's duplicate profile process rather than leaving it live. Reviews also play a part in how your new location builds trust, which we cover in our post on [reviews as local entity signals](/digital-news/reviews-local-entity-signals-vancouver/).

## Update Google Ads and paid channels

Your ads can keep promoting the old address long after your website has moved on. Two settings matter most.

First, location assets. Google Ads says location asset data comes from Google Maps and your linked Business Profile, and that successful updates there flow into linked Ads accounts. Edit the address in the Business Profile, not in Ads, then check that the asset shows the new location before you rely on it.

Second, location targeting. If your campaigns target a radius around the old office, recentre it on the new one and review whether the area still matches where your customers are. Our guide to [Google Ads location targeting in Vancouver](/digital-news/google-ads-location-targeting-vancouver/) explains the targeting settings that often catch people out after a move.

## Days 22 to 30: monitor, and know what to expect

The last week is for checking. Re-run the same rankings report you took as a baseline and compare calls, direction requests and website clicks from your Business Profile.

Expect some movement. Google says local results are mainly based on [relevance, distance and prominence](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091), and a new address changes distance for every searcher. Searchers near the new office may see you more often, and those near the old one less. Distance is part of how local results work, so this shift is expected rather than a penalty.

| Check | Where to look | What a problem looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Profile shows new address | Google Search and Maps | Old address or "pending" status after verification |
| No duplicate at old address | Search your business name on Maps | Two listings, or a new profile without your reviews |
| Website matches profile | Footer, contact page, schema | Old postal code anywhere on the site |
| Ads show correct location | Location assets in Google Ads | Location asset still pointing to the old office |
| Visibility trend | Geo-grid report vs baseline | Sharp drop across the new neighbourhood after 30 days |

### What this checklist will not do

This process removes the contradictions that make a move risky. It cannot guarantee your rankings recover to the same level, because your new address genuinely changes who you are closest to. Some directories will take longer than 30 days to update, and verification timing is up to Google. If you are moving to a virtual office or shared workspace, check Google's eligibility rules first, because a location that is not staffed during business hours may not qualify for a profile at all.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will I lose my Google reviews if I move my office?

Not usually. Google says reviews transfer automatically when you move and keep the same business name, with exceptions for categories such as hotels and golf courses. Creating a new profile instead of editing the old one is the common way businesses lose them.

### Do I need to verify my Google Business Profile again after moving?

Expect to. Google says a verified business that moves must verify again. Google picks the method, and standard verification reviews can take up to five business days.

### How long does it take for a new address to show on Google?

Google says edits usually appear in about 10 minutes but can take up to 30 days. If a postcard is involved, most codes arrive within 14 days, so plan your move announcement around that.
