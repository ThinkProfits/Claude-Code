# Google Business Profile Suspended? An Evidence Kit for Canadian Businesses

> **Quick answer:** If your Google Business Profile is suspended, fix whatever breaks Google's guidelines first, then gather documents that show the same business name and address as the profile, and submit a single appeal through Google's appeals tool. Google says appeals can only be submitted once, so the evidence you attach matters more than how fast you file.

A suspension is often noticed indirectly: the phone goes quiet, someone mentions they couldn't find you on Maps, and the dashboard says the profile has been suspended. When a Google Business Profile is suspended, the instinct is to file an appeal within the hour. We understand the urge, but a rushed appeal with mismatched documents can turn a fixable suspension into a long one.

This guide is the evidence kit we put together before touching the appeals tool for a Canadian business. It covers what a suspension is, the guideline problems that tend to trigger one, which documents to gather (with the Canadian versions of each), and how to file one clean appeal. It will not tell you how to force a reinstatement, because nobody outside Google can do that.

## What a suspension actually means

A suspension means Google has decided the profile, or the account managing it, breaks its rules. Google's help pages describe a few distinct situations, so confirm which one you are in first.

- **Profile suspended or disabled:** Google says it may suspend or disable Business Profiles that don't follow its guidelines. This is the case this kit is built for.
- **Google Account restricted:** if the account itself is restricted, every profile it manages is suspended and that account can't create or claim other profiles. Google's instructions are to lift the account restriction first, then appeal the profile.
- **Manager account removed:** when a manager (often a former agency or staff member) is removed, the locations themselves are not affected. Check this before assuming the business itself is in trouble.

The appeals tool shows the date of the decision, the reason, a link to the policy involved, and whether the decision can be appealed. Screenshot all of it, because that screen is the closest thing you will get to a diagnosis.

## Common triggers, mapped to Google's guidelines

Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) are the rulebook. Google doesn't publish a list of suspension reasons, so treat the table below as the places we look first, not a diagnosis.

| Area | What the guidelines say | What we usually find |
|---|---|---|
| Business name | The name must reflect the real-world name. Google says unnecessary information in the name "could result in the suspension" of the profile. | "Vancouver Plumbing Experts 24/7" on the profile, "Smith Plumbing Ltd." on the invoice. |
| Address | P.O. boxes, remote mailboxes and virtual offices aren't eligible. Co-working spaces need signage, customer visits and staff during business hours. | A virtual office or mail-drop address used to appear closer to downtown Vancouver. |
| Service-area setup | If you serve customers at their location and have no storefront, Google says you should hide your address. | A home-based trades business showing its home address as a storefront. |
| Phone and website | The phone must connect to that location and be under your direct control. The website can't redirect elsewhere, including to social media. | A call-tracking number nobody controls any more, or a website link that forwards to Facebook. |
| Duplicates | Only one profile per physical location. | An old profile from a previous owner or agency still live alongside the new one. |

Fix the problem on the profile, and in the real world where needed, before you appeal. Appealing a profile that still breaks the rule is a fast way to burn your one attempt.

## The evidence kit: what to gather before you appeal

Google's appeal guidance lists official business registration, a business licence, tax certificates and utility bills (such as electricity, phone, cable or internet) as examples of supporting evidence, with one tip that matters more than the rest: the business name and address on the documents should match the profile. For a Canadian business, these are the versions we gather.

- **Business registration:** your BC Registries record or federal incorporation documents. If the profile name differs from the registered corporate name, you also need proof that you trade under that name, such as the BC Registries registration for that business name.
- **Municipal business licence:** for example, a current City of Vancouver or City of Burnaby business licence showing the address on the profile. Expired licences invite questions you don't want.
- **Tax documents:** a CRA document that shows the business name, such as a GST/HST registration confirmation.
- **Utility bill:** a recent hydro, phone or internet bill in the business name at the profile address. A bill in the owner's personal name proves less than you'd hope.
- **Photos of the location (our addition):** clear shots of permanent exterior signage, the entrance and the street, ideally with something that ties them to the address. Google's appeal page doesn't list photos, but its guidelines rely on signage as real-world proof in several places.

Before uploading, lay every document next to the profile and compare the name and address character by character. A different unit number, an old address or a missing "Ltd." is exactly the kind of gap an appeal reviewer can notice. ## How to file the appeal, step by step

These steps follow Google's [appeal instructions for Business Profiles](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13597551) as they read today.

1. **Sign in with the right account.** Open the Google Business Profile appeals tool and confirm you're signed in to the Google Account that owns or manages the suspended profile. Use the owner's account rather than an agency login so the email updates reach the business.
2. **Select the profile and the decision.** Choose the suspended profile, then the decision you want to appeal.
3. **Attach evidence and explain briefly.** Upload your strongest two or three documents with "Choose file" and use the text box to say what was wrong and what you changed. Three plain sentences beat three angry paragraphs.
4. **Submit once and wait.** Tick the confirmation box and select "Submit appeal." Google says reviews and decisions can take up to five business days, and it sends the decision by email; you can also check status in the appeals tool.

While you wait, don't create a new profile for the same business (Google explicitly warns against it) and don't file duplicate appeals for the same issue.

## If the appeal is denied

A denial is not always the end. Google's help pages say you may be able to request a further review with new evidence through the local appeals contact form. The key words are "new evidence": resubmitting the same licence with a sterner explanation rarely changes the outcome.

Use the denial as a prompt to recheck the triggers above. A second request is most worth making when you have found something the first appeal missed, such as an old duplicate profile, a call-tracking number on the listing, or a name that still carried a keyword.

## Preventing the next suspension

Prevention is mostly about keeping the profile boring and accurate.

- **Treat the name as fixed:** keep it identical to your signage and registration, and resist adding a city or service keyword. The suspension risk is written into Google's guidelines.
- **Run edits through one person:** a single owner of profile changes avoids the agency, staff member and owner all "fixing" the address in the same week. ## FAQ

### Why was my Google Business Profile suspended?

Google says it suspends or disables profiles that don't follow its guidelines, and the appeals tool shows the specific policy linked to your decision. In our experience the usual causes are keywords added to the business name, a virtual office or mailbox address, a home address shown when it should be hidden, or a duplicate profile.

### How long does a Google Business Profile reinstatement take?

Google's appeal help page says reviews and decisions can take up to five business days, and the decision arrives by email. Treat that as a guide rather than a promise, and keep your website, phone line and other listings current so customers can still reach you while the profile is down.

### Can I appeal a Google Business Profile suspension more than once?

Google says appeals can only be submitted once and asks you not to send duplicates for the same issue. If the appeal is denied, its help pages say you may be able to request a further review through the local appeals contact form, but only with new evidence.

### Should I create a new profile while the old one is suspended?

No. Google explicitly says not to create a new Business Profile for the same business. A second profile for the same location also breaks the one-profile-per-location rule.

### What documents does Google accept for a Business Profile appeal?

Google lists examples such as official business registration, a business licence, tax certificates and utility bills (electricity, phone, cable or internet). For a Canadian business, that usually means a BC Registries record, a municipal business licence, a CRA document and a recent utility bill, all showing the same name and address as the profile.
