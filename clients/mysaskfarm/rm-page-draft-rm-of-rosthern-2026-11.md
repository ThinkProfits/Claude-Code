### H2: What farmland sells for in the RM of Rosthern

No single price per acre fits the whole RM. Rosthern land is priced on what it can grow, how it's laid out, and how close it sits to Highway 11, the main route between Saskatoon and Prince Albert. Much of the RM is good, Class 2 and 3 land, so soil quality alone won't separate one quarter from the next. The details do.

The main factors buyers and appraisers weigh here:

- **Soil and productivity:** soil class, stones, slope, drainage and the land's cropping history
- **Access to Highway 11:** quick access to the highway and to grain delivery points matters for hauling
- **River land:** parcels near the North or South Saskatchewan River can include breaks, slopes or bush that don't crop like the flat land above them
- **Size and access:** a full quarter on a good grid road draws a different buyer than a small or landlocked parcel
- **Buildings:** a yard site with a home, shop or bins adds value on top of the land itself

#### H3: Saskatchewan farmland values are still climbing

Across Saskatchewan, average cultivated farmland values rose 15.7% in 2023, 13.1% in 2024 and 9.4% in 2025, according to [Farm Credit Canada](https://www.fcc-fac.ca/en/knowledge/economics/farmland-values-report). Growth in 2025 was strongest in the North West and North East regions (12.1% each) and slowest in West Central (4.8%). For FCC's per-acre averages by region, read [How Much Is an Acre of Farmland Worth in Saskatchewan?](https://mysaskfarm.com/blog/how-much-farmland-acre/), and for the bigger picture, [Saskatchewan Farmland Prices: Are Land Values Still Going Up?](https://mysaskfarm.com/blog/saskatchewan-farmland-prices/)

#### H3: Assessed value vs. market value

Your SAMA assessment is not a sale price. SAMA values farmland on its productive capacity, using a formula based on soil productivity ratings, economic factors and a provincial factor.

Assessments are also historical. The 2025 revaluation reflects values as of January 1, 2023, and those values stay in place through 2028. Provincial farmland values have risen every year since that date, so in most of the province, assessed value now trails what land actually sells for.

### H2: FAQs About RM of Rosthern

#### H3: Where is the RM of Rosthern?

The RM of Rosthern No. 403 is in central Saskatchewan, starting about 35 km north of Saskatoon on Highway 11. The South Saskatchewan River forms its eastern boundary, and it borders the RM of Corman Park to the south, the RM of Laird to the west and the RM of Duck Lake to the north. The RM office is in the town of Rosthern.

#### H3: Are Rosthern and Hague in the RM of Rosthern?

Yes. The towns of Rosthern and Hague both sit inside the RM, along with the hamlets of Neuanlage, Gruenthal, Blumenthal and Carlton. The towns have their own councils, while the RM covers the farmland and rural roads around them.

#### H3: Is there farmland for sale in the RM of Rosthern?

Yes, though supply changes week to week. Listings range from small parcels to full quarter sections and farms with yard sites. Farmland across Saskatchewan has been in short supply in recent years, as many retiring farmers rent their land out instead of selling.

#### H3: How much is farmland worth in the RM of Rosthern?

It depends on soil productivity, parcel size, access, buildings and how much of a parcel is cultivated. River-break land and flat crop land on the same quarter can be worth very different amounts. A local appraisal or recent comparable sales give the most reliable figure for a specific parcel.

#### H3: What are average crop yields in the RM of Rosthern?

From 2016 to 2025, the RM of Rosthern averaged about 42.2 bushels per acre for canola, 52.8 for spring wheat, 67.7 for barley, 91.1 for oats and 42.3 for field peas. Yields vary with moisture: canola averaged 15.0 bushels in the 2021 drought and 50.5 in 2025.

#### H3: How is farmland assessed in the RM of Rosthern?

The Saskatchewan Assessment Management Agency (SAMA) assesses farmland on its productive capacity, not its sale price. The 2025 revaluation reflects values as of January 1, 2023, and applies through 2028, so assessed values usually sit below current market value.

#### H3: When is the best time to buy or sell farmland near Rosthern?

Winter, from November to March, is typically the busiest season for farmland sales in Saskatchewan. Farmers are out of the field and planning for the coming year. Read more in [The Best Time to Sell Farmland in Saskatchewan](https://mysaskfarm.com/blog/best-time-sell-farmland-saskatchewan/).
