## What farmland sells for in the RM of Rosthern

With so much of the RM rated Class 2 and 3, buyers here look hard at the details: how much of a quarter is actually in crop, how close it sits to Highway 11, and whether the river has taken a bite out of it.

What moves the price on a Rosthern quarter:

- **Cultivated acres:** a quarter that's all crop land sells differently from one with bush, sloughs or river breaks
- **River land:** parcels along the North or South Saskatchewan River can include breaks and slopes that don't crop like the flat land above them
- **Access to Highway 11:** quick access to the main Saskatoon to Prince Albert route makes hauling easier
- **Buildings:** a yard site with a home, shop or bins adds value on top of the land itself

## Rosthern land and crop yields

Rosthern's yields back up its land ratings. From 2016 to 2025, the RM averaged 42.2 bushels an acre for canola and 52.8 for spring wheat, ahead of Corman Park, Vanscoy and Blucher over the same years.

Dry years still hurt. The 2021 drought cut Rosthern canola to 15.0 bushels an acre, while 2025 came in at 50.5, the RM's best canola crop of the decade.

## FAQs About the RM of Rosthern

### Where is the RM of Rosthern?

The RM of Rosthern No. 403 is in central Saskatchewan, about 35 km north of Saskatoon on Highway 11. The South Saskatchewan River forms its eastern boundary, and it borders the RM of Corman Park to the south, the RM of Laird to the west and the RM of Duck Lake to the north.

### Are Rosthern and Hague in the RM of Rosthern?

Yes. The towns of Rosthern and Hague both sit inside the RM, along with the hamlets of Neuanlage, Gruenthal, Blumenthal and Carlton. The towns have their own councils, while the RM looks after the farmland and rural roads around them. The RM's population grew 7.5% between 2016 and 2021.

### What is the land like in the RM of Rosthern?

Mostly Class 2 and 3 agricultural land under the Canada Land Inventory, which the RM's Official Community Plan calls prime farmland. The exceptions sit along the North and South Saskatchewan rivers, where breaks, slopes and bush can take a share of a quarter out of crop.
