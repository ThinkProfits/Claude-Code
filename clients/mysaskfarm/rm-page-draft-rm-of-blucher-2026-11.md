### H2: What farmland sells for in the RM of Blucher

No single price per acre fits the whole RM. Blucher land is priced on what it can grow, how close it sits to Saskatoon, and how easily you can get to it on Highway 5 or 16 `[VERIFY: 16]`. A quarter near Clavet, a short drive from the city, can draw a different crowd than one in the south of the RM near Allan.

The main factors buyers and appraisers weigh here:

- **Soil and productivity:** soil class, stones, slope, drainage and the land's cropping history
- **Distance to Saskatoon:** parcels closer to the city can draw more buyers, including some who aren't farming the land
- **Access:** paved highway and good grid-road access matter for hauling grain and moving equipment
- **Size and shape:** a full, square quarter draws a different buyer than a small or awkwardly shaped parcel
- **Buildings:** a yard site with a home, shop or bins adds value on top of the land itself

#### H3: Saskatchewan farmland values are still climbing

Across Saskatchewan, average cultivated farmland values rose 15.7% in 2023, 13.1% in 2024 and 9.4% in 2025, according to [Farm Credit Canada](https://www.fcc-fac.ca/en/knowledge/economics/farmland-values-report). Growth in 2025 was strongest in the North West and North East regions (12.1% each) and slowest in West Central (4.8%). For FCC's per-acre averages by region, read [How Much Is an Acre of Farmland Worth in Saskatchewan?](https://mysaskfarm.com/blog/how-much-farmland-acre/), and for the bigger picture, [Saskatchewan Farmland Prices: Are Land Values Still Going Up?](https://mysaskfarm.com/blog/saskatchewan-farmland-prices/)

#### H3: Assessed value vs. market value

Your SAMA assessment is not a sale price. SAMA values farmland on its productive capacity, using a formula based on soil productivity ratings, economic factors and a provincial factor.

Assessments are also historical. The 2025 revaluation reflects values as of January 1, 2023, and those values stay in place through 2028. Provincial farmland values have risen every year since that date, so in most of the province, assessed value now trails what land actually sells for.

### H2: FAQs About RM of Blucher

#### H3: Where is the RM of Blucher?

The RM of Blucher No. 343 is in central Saskatchewan, starting about 10 minutes southeast of Saskatoon. It surrounds the town of Allan and the villages of Bradwell and Clavet, and borders the RMs of Corman Park, Dundurn and Aberdeen `[VERIFY: neighbours]`. The RM office is in Bradwell.

#### H3: Is there farmland for sale in the RM of Blucher?

Yes, though supply changes week to week. Listings range from pasture and small parcels to full quarter sections and farms with yard sites. Farmland across Saskatchewan has been in short supply in recent years, as many retiring farmers rent their land out instead of selling.

#### H3: How much is farmland worth in the RM of Blucher?

It depends on soil productivity, parcel size, access, buildings and distance to Saskatoon. Land near Clavet and the city can draw extra buyers, so prices vary across the RM. A local appraisal or recent comparable sales give the most reliable figure for a specific parcel.

#### H3: What is the RM of Blucher known for?

Farming and potash. Along with grain and oilseed land, the RM is home to Nutrien's Allan and Patience Lake potash mines, plus fertilizer and grain-handling facilities at Clavet and both CN and CP rail lines.

#### H3: What are average crop yields in the RM of Blucher?

From 2016 to 2025, the RM of Blucher averaged about 36.0 bushels per acre for canola, 45.4 for spring wheat, 58.2 for barley, 71.2 for oats and 35.8 for field peas. Yields vary with moisture: canola averaged 12.9 bushels in the 2021 drought and 46.3 in 2025.

#### H3: How is farmland assessed in the RM of Blucher?

The Saskatchewan Assessment Management Agency (SAMA) assesses farmland on its productive capacity, not its sale price. The 2025 revaluation reflects values as of January 1, 2023, and applies through 2028, so assessed values usually sit below current market value.

#### H3: When is the best time to buy or sell farmland near Blucher?

Winter, from November to March, is typically the busiest season for farmland sales in Saskatchewan. Farmers are out of the field and planning for the coming year. Read more in [The Best Time to Sell Farmland in Saskatchewan](https://mysaskfarm.com/blog/best-time-sell-farmland-saskatchewan/).
