# Furnace Blowing Cold Air? Fixes in Surrey, BC

> **Quick Answer (Preformatted block):** If your furnace is blowing cold air, first check that the thermostat is set to Heat and the fan is set to Auto, not On, and give the furnace a minute or two to warm up. Then check for a clogged air filter, blocked outside vents or a backed-up condensate drain, since each one can make the furnace shut off its burners while the fan keeps running. If the air is still cold, the burners are likely not lighting or not staying lit, and a licensed gas fitter should take a look.

## Check the Thermostat First

Most cold-air calls start and end at the thermostat, so rule it out before anything else.

### Fan Set to On Instead of Auto

Look at the fan setting on your thermostat. When it's set to **On**, the blower runs all the time, including between heating cycles when the burners are off, so you feel room-temperature air that seems cold. Switch the fan to **Auto** so the blower only runs while the furnace is actually heating. It's a common culprit after someone changes the setting over the summer.

### Mode, Setpoint and Batteries

Make sure the thermostat is set to **Heat**, not Cool, Off or a fan-only mode, and that the setpoint is a few degrees above the current room temperature. If the screen is blank or dim, replace the batteries, since a thermostat with low batteries can stop sending a signal to the furnace. Smart thermostats may also be following a schedule that holds the temperature lower than you expect. Our guide to [comparing different thermostats](https://www.johnsadler.ca/blog/comparing-different-thermostats/) explains how the common models behave.

### Give It a Minute to Warm Up

A furnace runs through a startup sequence before warm air arrives. The burners light first and heat the heat exchanger, and then the blower starts, so the first few seconds of air can feel cool while the ducts warm up. If the air turns warm within a minute or two, the furnace is working normally. If it stays cool, keep going with the checks below.

## Check the Air Filter

A clogged filter is one of the most common causes of a furnace that starts out warm and then turns cold.

[IMAGE: Homeowner sliding a clean filter into a furnace filter slot | alt: Replacing a dirty filter on a furnace blowing cold air | file: furnace-filter-replacement-surrey.jpg]

When dust blocks the filter, not enough air moves across the heat exchanger and the furnace gets too hot inside. A safety device called the high limit switch then shuts off the burners to protect the equipment, but the blower keeps running to cool things down, which sends cool air through the vents. The furnace may restart, overheat and shut off again, a pattern called short cycling. Check the filter, and if it looks grey or you can't see light through it, replace it. Natural Resources Canada recommends that homeowners [inspect, clean or change furnace filters regularly](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/stories/spotlight-energy-efficiency/guide-maintaining-your-home-heating-cooling-system) and follow the owner's manual for how often. Our step-by-step guide on [how to replace your furnace filter](https://www.johnsadler.ca/blog/furnace-filter/) shows how to find the right size.

If a new filter doesn't solve it, or the furnace keeps switching on and off, our post on [why a furnace keeps shutting off](https://www.johnsadler.ca/blog/why-does-my-furnace-keep-shutting-off/) covers other airflow causes in more detail.

## Check the Vents and the Condensate Drain

High-efficiency furnaces have safety switches that stop the burners when the venting or drainage isn't working.

Most furnaces installed in the Lower Mainland in the last 20 years are high-efficiency models with two white plastic pipes running outside: one brings in combustion air and one carries exhaust out. If leaves, a bird's nest, ice or a snowdrift blocks either pipe, a pressure switch tells the furnace not to light, and the blower may run without any heat. Walk outside and check that both pipe openings are clear.

These furnaces also produce water as they run, which drains away through a small tube to a floor drain or pump. If that drain line clogs or freezes, water backs up and a safety switch can shut off the burners. Look for standing water around the base of the furnace or a full condensate pump. Our guide on [why a furnace leaks water](https://www.johnsadler.ca/blog/furnace-leaking-water/) explains the drain system and what to do if it's backed up.

## When the Burners Won't Light or Won't Stay Lit

If the thermostat, filter, vents and drain all check out, the problem is usually in the burner or ignition system, and these are jobs for a licensed gas fitter.

[IMAGE: Close-up of a furnace burner assembly with the flames lit | alt: Gas furnace burners lit during a furnace repair in Surrey, BC | file: furnace-burners-lit-repair.jpg]

- **A dirty flame sensor:** the flame sensor is a thin metal rod that confirms the burners are lit. When it's coated with residue, the furnace lights, can't confirm the flame and shuts off within a few seconds, often trying several times before locking out. Our technicians clean or replace it as part of a repair.
- **A failed igniter or pilot:** most modern furnaces use an electronic igniter that glows to light the gas, and these wear out over time. Older furnaces with a standing pilot light will blow cold air if the pilot goes out, and if it won't stay lit, the part that senses it may have failed.
- **A gas supply or gas valve problem:** if other gas appliances in the home aren't working either, the issue may be the gas supply. If they are working, the furnace's gas valve or control board may have failed, which a technician can test.
- **A control board lockout:** after several failed attempts to light, many furnaces lock out for a period of time. A flashing light behind the furnace's viewing window often shows a fault code that helps a technician find the cause quickly.

Don't open the burner compartment or adjust gas parts yourself. If you smell gas, leave your home right away, leaving the door open behind you, and once outside call the FortisBC emergency line at [1-800-663-9911](https://www.fortisbc.com/safety-outages/energy-safety/natural-gas-safety/gas-leaks-and-odour) or 911. If your carbon monoxide alarm sounds, get everyone outside and call 911.

## Cold Air From Some Vents but Not Others

If most rooms are warm but one or two vents blow cool air, the furnace is probably working and the problem is in the ductwork.

Leaky or disconnected ducts in an attic, crawl space or garage can lose a lot of heat before the air reaches the room, so the air feels cool by the time it arrives. Closed or blocked supply registers and return grilles can also starve parts of the home of warm air. Check that registers are open and not covered by furniture or rugs. If the registers are open, a technician can check whether a duct has come loose or needs sealing.

## Do You Have a Heat Pump as Well?

Many homes in Surrey and Langley now pair a heat pump with a gas furnace, and the air from a heat pump feels cooler than air from a furnace.

A heat pump delivers warm air at a lower temperature than a gas furnace, so the air at the vent can feel lukewarm even when it's heating properly. In cold, damp weather, the heat pump also runs a defrost cycle to clear frost off the outdoor unit, and the air inside may briefly feel cool while it does. In a dual fuel system, the furnace should take over when it gets colder outside. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why is my furnace blowing cold air instead of hot air?

The most common causes are the thermostat fan set to On instead of Auto, a clogged air filter that makes the furnace overheat and shut off its burners, or a blocked vent pipe or condensate drain. If those all check out, the burners may not be lighting or staying lit because of a dirty flame sensor, a failed igniter or a gas valve problem, which needs a licensed gas fitter.

### Why does my furnace blow cold air for a minute before it warms up?

That's usually normal. The burners light first and heat the heat exchanger, then the blower starts, so the first air through the ducts can feel cool. If the air is still cool after a few minutes, check the filter and the thermostat settings.

### Can a dirty filter make a furnace blow cold air?

Yes. A clogged filter restricts airflow, the furnace overheats, and the high limit switch shuts off the burners while the blower keeps running. Replacing the filter often fixes it, but if the furnace keeps shutting off afterwards, have a technician check it.

### Should I reset my furnace if it keeps blowing cold air?

You can turn the furnace off at its power switch, wait a minute and turn it back on once. If it lights and then shuts off again, stop resetting it, because repeated resets can hide a problem such as a dirty flame sensor or a blocked vent that needs a proper repair.

### Is it safe to keep running a furnace that is blowing cold air?

It's safe to run the fan while you check the thermostat and filter, but don't keep resetting a furnace that won't stay lit. If you smell gas or your carbon monoxide alarm sounds, leave the home and call the FortisBC emergency line or 911 from outside.
