# Navien Furnace Error Codes Explained: NPF Hydro Furnace and NDF Gas Furnace

> **Quick Answer (Preformatted block):** Navien furnace error codes work differently by model. The NPF hydro furnace shows an "E" code on its front control panel, such as E003 (ignition failure) or E302 (low water level), and resets with the Back button. The NDF gas furnace uses a red status light that flashes a set number of times, such as 1 flash for an open high limit switch. If a code returns after a reset, call a licensed technician, and if you smell gas, leave and call FortisBC at 1-800-663-9911 or 911.

Navien makes two very different furnaces. The NPF is a hydro furnace: it heats water with a gas burner, then uses that water to warm the air moving through your ducts. The NDF is a traditional high-efficiency gas furnace. Because they work differently, they report problems differently, so a Navien furnace error code only makes sense once you know which model you have.

This guide covers both, using the code tables from Navien's own installation and operation manuals. ## Gas Safety Comes First

Both furnaces burn natural gas, so some situations call for leaving the house rather than reading a code.

- **If you smell gas (a rotten egg odour):** don't light anything, touch switches or use a phone inside; go outside. FortisBC lists its 24-hour emergency line as 1-800-663-9911, and you can also call 911, as outlined on its [gas leaks and odour page](https://www.fortisbc.com/safety-outages/energy-safety/natural-gas-safety/gas-leaks-and-odour).
- **If your carbon monoxide alarm sounds:** get everyone outside and call 911.

Everything below assumes there's no gas smell and no alarm. Burners, gas valves, venting and wiring are for a licensed gas fitter only.

## NPF Hydro Furnace: How to Read and Reset a Code

When the NPF detects a problem, its front control panel flashes red and shows an "E" code, sometimes with a two-digit sub code. Navien's manual says to press the Back button to reset the furnace, and to refer to the code table if that doesn't solve it.

One reset is reasonable, especially after a power outage. If the code comes back, write down the code, sub code and model number (for example, NPF700-080H) before calling.

### Ignition, Flame and Overheat Codes

These codes mean the burner couldn't light, lost its flame or got too hot.

- **E003, Ignition failure:** no flame was detected. Navien says to check that the main gas supply is open, that the exhaust isn't blocked or damaged, and that the condensate drain isn't clogged.
- **E004, False flame:** the flame detector isn't reading correctly. Navien notes it clears on its own if the furnace then runs normally.
- **E012, Flame loss during combustion:** the flame went out while running. Navien points to the supply (intake) and exhaust pipes.
- **E016 and E031, Overheating of the heat exchanger or burner:** an overheat control tripped during combustion.
- **E030, Abnormal exhaust gas temperature (Lock Out), and E046, Abnormal limit control operation on the heat exchanger:** Navien's instruction for both is to stop operating the furnace and get it serviced.

You can safely check that the outside intake and exhaust terminals are clear of snow and debris and that the condensate line isn't frozen or kinked. If E030 or E046 appears, don't keep resetting it.

### Water Level and Leak Codes

Because the NPF heats with water, it has its own internal water supply, and several codes relate to it.

- **E302, Low water level:** the furnace's internal water tank isn't full enough. On units set to manual fill, Navien's manual tells you to open the water inlet cover on the lower-front cover and fill the tank.
- **E351, Abnormal water fill:** on units with automatic fill, the fill valve isn't working. Navien's first check is whether the water valve to the furnace is open.
- **E311, Abnormal water level:** the water level sensor isn't working properly.
- **E360, Abnormal cover opening:** the water inlet cover or lower-front cover is open. Make sure both are properly closed.
- **E799, Leak detected:** the furnace has sensed a leak. Look for water around the base, then call.

### Airflow, Filter and Maintenance Alert Codes

The NPF moves air with an ECM blower motor, which Navien calls the circulator, and it also tracks replacement dates for add-on air quality accessories.

- **E810 and E811, Abnormal circulator operation:** the blower motor isn't running. Navien lists checking the filter, the supply and return ducts, and for anything caught inside the furnace.
- **E822, Overcooling of the air conditioning coil:** in cooling mode, supply air is too cold. Navien says to check the evaporator coil, the duct filter and the cooling airflow setting.
- **E860, Replacement alerts:** sub code 01 means the circulator filter needs replacing, 02 the humidifier pad, and 03 the UV light. Replace the item, then reset the furnace.

A dirty filter is a common cause of airflow codes. Our guide to [changing your furnace filter](https://www.johnsadler.ca/blog/furnace-filter/) explains how often to check it.

## NDF Gas Furnace: Reading the Flashing Status Light

The NDF doesn't show letters and numbers. Its control board has a red status light that stays on during normal operation and flashes a set number of times to report a fault. Navien's manual lists these patterns:

| Red status light | What it means |
|---|---|
| Off | No power to the control, or a control fault |
| On (steady) | Normal operation |
| 1 flash | High limit switch open |
| 2 flashes | Pressure switch open with inducer on |
| 3 flashes | Pressure switch closed with inducer off |
| 4 flashes | Control in 1 hour lockout |
| 5 flashes | L1/Neutral polarity problem |
| 6 flashes | Too many limit trips within one call for heat |
| 7 flashes | Pressure switch cycle lockout |
| 8 flashes | Lockout due to too many flame dropouts |
| 10 flashes | Flame present with gas valve off |

Source: Navien NDF Installation & Operation Manual, section 18.5, from [Navien's NDF downloads page](https://www.navieninc.com/series/ndf/downloads).

The board also stores the last five fault codes, which a technician can replay with the push button on the control. Navien's manual warns not to run the furnace's heating unless its doors are installed, so only count the flashes if you can see the light without removing a panel.

Limit codes (1 and 6 flashes) often trace back to restricted airflow, and Navien's manual notes that dirty filters lead to higher than normal operating temperatures. Pressure switch codes (2, 3 and 7 flashes) usually involve the venting, the inducer fan or the condensate drain. Any code involving flame (8 or 10 flashes) or polarity (5 flashes) needs a technician right away.

## What You Can Safely Check, and When to Call

For either furnace, a few checks are safe for any homeowner:

1. **Reset once.** On the NPF, press the Back button. Note whether the same code returns.
2. **Check the thermostat, power switch and breaker.** A furnace with no power shows no code at all.
3. **Check the filter.** Replace it if it's dirty, since restricted airflow is behind many limit and blower codes.
4. **Check the outside vents and condensate line.** Clear snow or debris and look for a frozen or kinked drain line.
5. **Confirm the gas valve is open.** The handle should run in line with the pipe.

Call for service when a code returns after a reset, when the NPF shows E030 or E046, or when an NDF shows a lockout, flame or polarity code. Navien's NDF manual also recommends annual cleaning and inspection by a trained technician.

## Get Your Navien Furnace Running Again

The NPF hydro furnace reports problems with "E" codes, while the NDF gas furnace uses flash counts on a status light. In both cases, one reset plus a look at power, the filter, the vents and the gas valve covers what a homeowner can safely do. A code that keeps returning means a part needs testing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I reset a Navien hydro furnace?

Press the Back button on the front control panel. That clears the current error so the furnace can try to restart. If the same code returns, a technician needs to find and fix the cause.

### What does E302 mean on a Navien hydro furnace?

E302 means the water tank inside the furnace is low. On units set to manual fill, the manual says to open the water inlet cover on the lower-front cover and top up the tank. If the code keeps returning, have the furnace checked for a leak or a fill problem.

### What do the flashing lights on a Navien gas furnace mean?

The NDF gas furnace's red status light flashes a set number of times to report a fault. For example, 1 flash means the high limit switch is open, 4 flashes means the control is in a one-hour lockout, and 8 flashes means a lockout from too many flame dropouts. A steady light means normal operation.

### Why is my Navien furnace asking me to replace the filter?

On the NPF hydro furnace, code E860 with sub code 01 is a reminder that the circulator filter is due for replacement. Sub codes 02 and 03 are reminders for the humidifier pad and UV light. Replace the item, then reset the furnace to clear the alert.
