## First, Work Out What Isn't Running

Start by noticing which parts of the system are dead, because that tells you where to look.

A central AC has two halves: the indoor furnace or air handler, which runs the blower, and the outdoor unit, which holds the compressor and fan. Set the thermostat to Cool a few degrees below room temperature and listen at both:

- **Nothing runs at all:** the thermostat isn't calling for cooling, or the indoor equipment has lost power or tripped a safety switch. Start with the thermostat and indoor power checks below.
- **The indoor fan runs but the outdoor unit is silent:** the outdoor unit has lost power or a part inside it has failed. Skip to the outdoor unit checks.
- **The outdoor unit hums or clicks but the fan and compressor don't start:** this usually points to a failed capacitor or contactor, which is a job for a technician.

If both halves run and the air is simply warm, that's a different problem. Our guide to [why your AC is not blowing cold air](https://www.johnsadler.ca/blog/ac-not-blowing-cold-air/) covers those causes.

## Check the Thermostat

The thermostat is the switch that tells the whole system to start, so it's the first thing to rule out.

### Blank Screen or Dead Batteries

If the display is blank or dim, replace the batteries, even if the thermostat is also wired to the furnace. Many thermostats lose their settings or stop sending a signal when the batteries run low. Smart thermostats that draw power from the furnace can also go blank if the furnace has lost power, so a dark screen sometimes points back to the furnace switch or breaker.

### Wrong Mode or Setpoint

Make sure the mode is set to **Cool**, not Heat, Auto or Off, and that the setpoint is at least a few degrees below the current room temperature. Natural Resources Canada suggests a cooling setting [in the range of 22 to 25°C](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/energy-efficiency/energy-star-canada/about/energy-star-announcements/publications/air-conditioning-your-home/6051), so a setpoint of 24°C on a 23°C day won't start anything. If a schedule is programmed, check that it isn't holding a higher temperature for the current time of day.

### The Built-In Delay

Most thermostats and outdoor units have a protective delay of a few minutes before the compressor restarts. If you've just changed the setting or reset a breaker, wait five minutes before assuming something is wrong. Our post on [comparing different thermostats](https://www.johnsadler.ca/blog/comparing-different-thermostats/) explains how common models behave.

## Check the Power: Breakers, Disconnect and Furnace Switch

A central AC usually has more than one power supply, and any one of them being off stops the system.

[IMAGE: Electrical panel with breakers labelled furnace and AC | alt: Electrical panel breakers for a furnace and AC unit in a Surrey home | file: ac-furnace-breakers-panel.jpg]

1. **Check the breakers in your electrical panel.** Look for breakers labelled AC, condenser, heat pump and furnace, since the indoor and outdoor units are usually on separate circuits. To reset a tripped breaker, set the thermostat to Off, switch the breaker fully off and then back on, and wait a few minutes before turning cooling back on.
2. **Check the outdoor disconnect.** This is the small grey box mounted on the wall beside the outdoor unit. It may have a pull-out block, a lever or its own breaker, and it's easy to leave off after maintenance or landscaping work.
3. **Check the furnace power switch.** Most furnaces have a switch that looks like a regular light switch on the wall or ceiling near the furnace. It's often bumped off by accident during spring cleaning or storage, and when it's off, the blower and the thermostat can lose power.

If a breaker trips again after you reset it, leave it off and call a technician. A breaker that keeps tripping is protecting your home from an electrical fault, and repeated resets can damage the equipment.

## Was the Power Shut Off for the Winter?

If your AC worked fine last summer and won't start on the first warm day, check whether someone switched it off for the season.

Natural Resources Canada advises that power to a central unit should be [shut off when the cooling season ends](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/energy-efficiency/energy-star-canada/about/energy-star-announcements/publications/air-conditioning-your-home/6051), and many homeowners and technicians do exactly that by pulling the disconnect or switching off the breaker. A heat pump is different, since it runs all year, but a standalone central AC may have been shut down in the fall. Check the disconnect and the breaker, and look for a cover or tarp on the outdoor unit that needs to come off before you start it.

## Check the Condensate Drain Safety Switch

Many systems have a float switch that shuts the AC off when the condensate drain backs up, which protects your ceilings and floors from water damage.

An air conditioner pulls a lot of moisture out of the air, and that water drains away through a pan and a small pipe near the furnace or air handler. If algae, dust or debris clogs the drain, water rises in the pan and lifts a small float switch that cuts power to the cooling system. Look for standing water in the pan under the indoor coil or a small switch clipped to the pan or drain line. If the pan is full, the drain needs to be cleared before the system will run. This is a common reason an AC in an attic or upper-floor air handler stops without warning.

## When the Outdoor Unit Won't Start

If the indoor fan runs but the outdoor unit stays silent or only hums, the problem is usually inside the outdoor unit, and these are not DIY repairs.

[IMAGE: Outdoor AC unit with the service panel closed and clear space around it | alt: Outdoor AC unit that won't start beside a South Surrey home | file: outdoor-ac-unit-not-starting.jpg]

- **A failed capacitor:** the capacitor gives the compressor and fan motor the boost they need to start. When it weakens, the unit may hum or click without starting, and it's one of the most common summer failures our technicians replace.
- **A worn or stuck contactor:** this relay switches the outdoor unit on when the thermostat calls for cooling. Pitted contacts or insects inside it can stop the signal from getting through.
- **A failed compressor or fan motor:** if the capacitor and contactor test fine, the motor itself may be the problem. Our technicians will explain whether a repair or a replacement makes more sense for the system's age.
- **A control board or wiring fault:** heat pumps in particular have control boards that can lock out the unit after a fault. A technician can read the fault code and find what triggered it.

Capacitors store a high-voltage charge even when the power is off, so never open the outdoor unit's service panel yourself. If you ever smell burning or see smoke from the unit, switch it off at the breaker and call 911 if there's any sign of fire.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why won't my AC turn on when the thermostat is set to cool?

The most common causes are dead thermostat batteries, a setpoint that isn't below the room temperature, a tripped breaker or a furnace power switch that's turned off. A full condensate pan can also trip a safety switch that stops the system. If all of those check out, a technician should test the capacitor, contactor and control board.

### Why is my outside AC unit not turning on but the inside fan is running?

The outdoor unit has likely lost power or has a failed part. Check its breaker in the panel and the disconnect box beside the unit. If both are on and the unit hums or stays silent, the capacitor or contactor has often failed, which needs a licensed technician.

### Why won't my AC turn on after a power outage?

Many systems wait a few minutes after power returns before the compressor restarts, so give it five to ten minutes. A power surge can also trip a breaker or reset a smart thermostat's settings. If the AC still won't start, check the breakers and thermostat, then call a technician.

### Should I keep resetting the breaker if my AC won't start?

No. Reset a tripped breaker once, with the thermostat off, and wait a few minutes before turning cooling back on. If it trips again, leave it off, because a breaker that keeps tripping is a sign of an electrical fault that needs a technician.
