## Start With the Simple Stuff: Thermostat and Power

Before assuming something has broken, rule out the settings and the power supply.

### 1. The Thermostat Is Set Wrong

Check that the thermostat is set to **Cool** and that the target temperature is lower than the room temperature. Then look at the fan setting. If the fan is set to **On**, the blower runs all the time, even when the outdoor unit has stopped cooling, so you get room-temperature air between cooling cycles. Setting the fan to **Auto** fixes this in a lot of homes, and fresh batteries help if the display is weak. Our guide to [comparing different thermostats](https://www.johnsadler.ca/blog/comparing-different-thermostats/) explains how the common types behave.

### 2. A Tripped Breaker or Outdoor Disconnect

If the indoor fan is running but the outdoor unit is silent, the outdoor unit may have lost power. Check your electrical panel for a tripped breaker labelled AC, heat pump or condenser, and check the disconnect box mounted on the wall beside the outdoor unit.

To reset a tripped breaker, turn the thermostat off first, switch the breaker fully off, then back on, and wait a few minutes before turning cooling back on. If the breaker trips again, leave it off and call a technician. A breaker that keeps tripping is protecting you from an electrical fault.

## Airflow Problems: The Most Common Cause

An AC not blowing cold air is very often an airflow problem, because the system can only cool the air that moves across its coil.

[IMAGE: Close-up of a clogged air filter next to a clean replacement | alt: Dirty furnace and AC air filter beside a new clean filter | file: dirty-ac-air-filter-replacement.jpg]

### 3. A Dirty Air Filter

The same filter that serves your furnace in winter serves your AC in summer. When it clogs with dust, pet hair and pollen, less air moves through the system and the air that does come out is not as cool. A badly clogged filter can also cause the indoor coil to freeze. Pull the filter out and hold it up to a light: if you can't see light through it, replace it. Our post on [choosing and changing your furnace filter](https://www.johnsadler.ca/blog/furnace-filter/) covers sizes and ratings.

### 4. Blocked Vents and Return Grilles

Make sure supply vents are open and return air grilles are not covered by furniture, rugs or curtains. Closing vents in unused rooms raises pressure in the ductwork and reduces overall airflow.

### 5. A Dirty or Blocked Outdoor Unit

The outdoor unit releases the heat pulled out of your home. If its coil is caked with cottonwood fluff, grass clippings or leaves, or shrubs are crowding it, it can't shed that heat. With the power off at the disconnect, clear debris and trim plants back so there's open space on all sides. You can gently rinse the coil with a garden hose, but never use a pressure washer, since it bends the thin metal fins.

## Ice on the Coil: Shut It Off and Let It Thaw

If you see frost or ice on the copper refrigerant line at the outdoor unit, or water pooling around the indoor unit, your evaporator coil has likely frozen.

[IMAGE: Frost on the copper refrigerant line at an outdoor unit | alt: Ice on an AC refrigerant line, a sign of a frozen evaporator coil | file: frozen-ac-refrigerant-line.jpg]

### 6. A Frozen Evaporator Coil

The evaporator coil sits above your furnace or inside the air handler. When airflow or refrigerant is low, the coil gets cold enough to freeze the moisture in the air, and the ice blocks airflow.

Here's what to do:

1. **Turn cooling off at the thermostat.** Running the system on a frozen coil strains the compressor, the most expensive part of the system to replace.
2. **Set the fan to On.** Running the blower on its own moves room air across the coil and speeds up thawing. Keep towels nearby in case the drain can't keep up with the meltwater.
3. **Check the filter and vents while you wait.** If a clogged filter caused the freeze, replacing it may be the whole fix. If the coil freezes again with a clean filter, call a technician.

Never chip at the ice or use a heat gun on the coil, since the fins and tubing are easy to damage.

## Problems That Need a Licensed Technician

If the settings, power, filter and outdoor unit all check out and your AC is still not blowing cold air, the problem is in the sealed system or the electrical parts. These are not DIY repairs.

### 7. Low Refrigerant or a Leak

Air conditioners don't "use up" refrigerant. If the charge is low, there's a leak somewhere, and topping it up without finding the leak just means paying for the same problem again. Signs include hissing sounds, ice on the lines and a system that runs for long stretches without cooling.

In British Columbia, only an approved person may service air conditioning or refrigeration equipment, under the provincial [Ozone Depleting Substances and Other Halocarbons Regulation](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/387_99). That means trade credentials plus an approved environmental awareness course, so refrigerant work belongs with a qualified technician, not a handy neighbour with a recharge kit.

### 8. A Failing Capacitor or Compressor

A weak capacitor is a common summer failure: the outdoor unit may hum or click without starting, or the fan spins while the air stays warm. Capacitors hold high voltage even with the power off, so only a technician should test or replace them. A compressor problem is more serious, and our technicians will explain whether repair or replacement makes more sense for your system's age.

### 9. Heat Pump Reversing Valve Problems

[IMAGE: Heat pump outdoor unit on a pad with clear space around it | alt: Heat pump outdoor unit with cleared space for airflow at a Surrey home | file: heat-pump-outdoor-unit-clearance-surrey.jpg]

A heat pump uses a reversing valve to switch between heating and cooling. If that valve or its control sticks, a heat pump set to Cool can keep moving heat into your home instead of out of it. If your heat pump blows warm air in cooling mode with the right settings, a technician will check this valve. Our post on [heat pump vs. AC units](https://www.johnsadler.ca/blog/heat-pump-vs-ac-unit/) explains how the two systems differ.

### 10. Leaky or Undersized Ductwork

Ducts that run through a hot attic or crawl space can leak cool air or pick up heat before it reaches your rooms. If one or two rooms are always warm while the rest of the house is fine, ductwork is a likely suspect.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why is my AC running but not cooling the house?

The most common reasons are a fan set to On instead of Auto, a clogged air filter, or a dirty outdoor coil. If the indoor fan runs but the outdoor unit is silent, check the breaker and the disconnect box beside the outdoor unit. If those are fine, the cause is likely low refrigerant, a frozen coil or a failed capacitor, which need a licensed technician.

### Should I turn off my AC if it's not blowing cold air?

Yes, if you see ice on the lines or the outdoor unit hums without starting. Running a system with a frozen coil or a failing electrical part strains the compressor, the costliest part to replace. Switch cooling off, run the fan on its own if the coil is frozen, and book a service call.

### How long does it take a frozen AC coil to thaw?

Most frozen coils thaw in a few hours with cooling off and the fan set to On, though heavy ice can take most of a day. If the coil freezes again within a day or two with a clean filter, a technician should check the refrigerant charge and blower.

### Why is my heat pump blowing warm air in cooling mode?

First confirm the thermostat is set to Cool with a target temperature below the room temperature. If the settings are correct, the cause can be a dirty filter, a dirty outdoor coil or low refrigerant, just as with a central AC. A heat pump also has a reversing valve that switches between heating and cooling, and if it sticks, the unit keeps heating until a technician repairs it.
