# Heating & Cooling Upgrades That Add Home Value

> **Quick Answer (Preformatted block):** The heating and cooling upgrades that increase home value in buyers' eyes are the ones that make a home comfortable year-round and easy to inspect: a heat pump or air conditioning, a high-efficiency furnace or boiler, a tankless water heater, radiant in-floor heating and smart thermostat controls. Documented maintenance and permits matter just as much, because they show buyers the system has been looked after.

If you are getting ready to sell in Surrey, BC, or planning a renovation you want to pay off later, your mechanical room deserves a look. Buyers and home inspectors spend real time on heating, cooling and hot water, because these are big-ticket systems that are expensive to replace. Heating and cooling upgrades that increase home value tend to do it in two ways: they make the home more appealing in a listing, and they remove reasons for a buyer to negotiate the price down after inspection.

Below are seven upgrades our team installs across South Surrey, White Rock, Cloverdale and Langley, with why buyers care about each. To be clear up front: no upgrade comes with a guaranteed return, and your local market has the final say.

## 1. A Heat Pump or Air Conditioning

Cooling has moved from "nice to have" to something many Lower Mainland buyers expect to see. After the 2021 heat dome and the hot summers since, a home without any cooling can feel like a project to a buyer touring it in July.

A [heat pump](https://www.johnsadler.ca/services/heating-cooling/heat-pumps/) is often the strongest choice here because it cools in summer and heats efficiently through most of a coastal BC winter. If your home already has a furnace you are happy with, central [air conditioning](https://www.johnsadler.ca/services/heating-cooling/air-conditioning/) is another route. Our guide on choosing between a heat pump and an AC unit explains the differences.

**Why buyers care:** Cooling is hard to add right after a move. A home that is comfortable in August takes one item off the buyer's to-do list.

## 2. A High-Efficiency Furnace

If your furnace is 15 to 20 years old, a buyer's inspector will likely flag it as nearing the end of its life. That note often turns into a price conversation.

Modern high-efficiency gas furnaces burn far less fuel to produce the same heat as older mid-efficiency models. The number to look for is the AFUE rating, and our post on AFUE ratings explained breaks down what those percentages mean in plain terms. Our team handles [furnace installation and replacement](https://www.johnsadler.ca/services/heating-cooling/furnace-installation/) for brands including Lennox, Carrier, Trane and Napoleon.

**Why buyers care:** A newer furnace with a transferable warranty means the buyer is not budgeting for a replacement in year one.

## 3. A High-Efficiency or Combi Boiler

Many older homes in White Rock, Ocean Park and Crescent Beach heat with hot water boilers. Those systems are comfortable and quiet, but an aging cast-iron boiler can worry a buyer who has never owned one.

Replacing it with a modern condensing boiler, or a [combi boiler](https://www.johnsadler.ca/combi-boiler/) that handles both home heating and domestic hot water in one wall-mounted unit, frees up floor space and improves efficiency. As Navien Certified Service Specialists, we install and service Navien combi boilers regularly.

**Why buyers care:** A compact, modern boiler looks well cared for in a mechanical room, and one appliance doing two jobs is simple for a new owner to understand.

## 4. A Tankless Water Heater

A [tankless water heater](https://www.johnsadler.ca/services/plumbing/tankless-water-heaters/) heats water only when you turn on a tap, so there is no large storage tank to leak and no running out of hot water halfway through a shower. Tankless units also commonly last longer than conventional tanks with regular maintenance.

We are Navien specialists, and Navien tankless units are a popular choice in Surrey homes. We have already written about the resale angle in our post on upgrading to a tankless water heater, so here is the short version.

**Why buyers care:** Endless hot water is easy to sell to a family, and a wall-hung unit frees up the space where an old tank used to sit.

## 5. Radiant In-Floor Heating

Warm floors are one of those features buyers remember after a showing. [Radiant in-floor heating](https://www.johnsadler.ca/services/plumbing/radiant-in-floor-heating/) circulates warm water through tubing under the floor, giving even heat without vents or blowing air.

It is most practical to add during a renovation when floors are already open, such as a basement suite, bathroom or main-floor remodel. In-floor heating is usually supplied by a boiler, so it fits well with the upgrade in number 3.

**Why buyers care:** Radiant heat reads as a premium feature. It is quiet, it does not move dust around, and tiled floors stop feeling cold underfoot.

## 6. A Smart Thermostat and Zoning

A smart or programmable thermostat lets the owner set schedules and adjust the temperature from a phone, and some models can help with energy rebate eligibility. Our post comparing different thermostats covers the main types.

Zoning goes a step further by splitting the home into areas with their own temperature settings, which is handy for two-storey homes where the upstairs runs warm.

**Why buyers care:** Modern controls make the whole system look up to date, and zoning answers the hot-bedroom complaint before a buyer raises it.

## 7. A Newer Hot Water Tank and Documented Maintenance

Not every upgrade has to be a big one. If you are staying with a conventional tank and yours is close to 10 years old, [replacing the hot water tank](https://www.johnsadler.ca/services/plumbing/hot-water-tanks-installs-replacement/) before listing can head off an inspection flag. Some home insurers also now ask about tank age, which matters to the buyer arranging coverage.

Equally useful is a record showing every appliance has been serviced: annual furnace and boiler maintenance, tankless descaling, and any repairs. Our post on [when to replace a water heater](https://www.johnsadler.ca/blog/replace-water-heater/) explains how to read a tank's age from its serial number.

**Why buyers care:** A tidy folder of service invoices tells a buyer the home has been looked after, and it costs very little to put together.

## What Home Inspectors and Buyers Look At

Inspectors are not grading how fancy your system is. They check age, condition and safety, and buyers read that report line by line:

- **Age of each appliance:** Inspectors read the data plate on the furnace, boiler, water heater and outdoor unit. Anything near the end of its expected life usually gets a note recommending the buyer budget for replacement.
- **Venting and gas connections:** Corroded vents, improper clearances or older gas connectors are common flags.
- **Signs of leaks or rust:** Water in a tank's drain pan, rust at the base of a tank or staining around a boiler all suggest a problem is coming.
- **Permits for past work:** Buyers and their realtors increasingly ask whether gas appliances were installed under permit. More on that next.

## Keep Your Paperwork

The paperwork behind an upgrade can matter as much as the equipment. In BC, installing or replacing a gas appliance such as a furnace, boiler or water heater generally requires a gas permit from Technical Safety BC. When you hire a licensed gas contractor, the contractor applies for the permit on your behalf, and you can confirm one is in place using the lookup tool on [Technical Safety BC's homeowner gas permits page](https://www.technicalsafetybc.ca/apply-for/permits/homeowner-permits/homeowner-gas-permits).

Our licensed gas fitters pull permits for the [gas fitting](https://www.johnsadler.ca/services/gas-fitting/) and appliance installs we do (Technical Safety BC licence LGA0092486). Keep these in one folder for your buyer:

- **Permit numbers and inspection records:** These show the work was done to code and inspected. A buyer's lender or insurer may ask for them.
- **Installation invoices and model numbers:** These confirm the age and efficiency of each system without anyone having to guess from a data plate.
- **Warranty registration:** Many manufacturer warranties can transfer to a new owner, which is a real selling point. Check your warranty terms and keep the registration confirmation.
- **Rebate paperwork:** If you used a rebate on a heat pump or high-efficiency appliance, keep the approval. Program details change often, so check our [FortisBC rebates page](https://www.johnsadler.ca/fortisbc-rebates/) and confirm current eligibility before you plan an upgrade around one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does a heat pump increase home value?

A heat pump can make a home more attractive to buyers because it provides both heating and cooling in one system. How much it affects the sale price depends on the local market, the age of the unit and the rest of the home, so no specific dollar increase can be promised.

### Does air conditioning add value to a home?

Air conditioning has become a common request from buyers in the Lower Mainland since the 2021 heat dome. It can make a home easier to sell, particularly for listings shown during summer. Any effect on price varies by neighbourhood, so ask a local realtor how often comparable homes in your area include cooling.

### Should I replace my furnace before selling my house?

If the furnace is over 15 years old, needs frequent repairs or is likely to be flagged by an inspector, replacing it before listing can prevent a price negotiation later. If it is under 10 years old, runs reliably and has been serviced every year, a tune-up and a copy of the service records are usually enough.

### Does a tankless water heater add value to a home?

A tankless water heater appeals to buyers because it supplies continuous hot water, saves floor space and removes the risk of a storage tank leaking. Tankless units commonly last 15 to 20 years with regular descaling, compared with roughly 8 to 12 years for a conventional tank.

### What paperwork should I keep for heating upgrades when selling a home?

Keep the gas permit number and inspection record for each gas appliance, along with the installation invoice, model and serial numbers, and warranty registration. In BC, gas appliance installs generally need a permit from Technical Safety BC.
