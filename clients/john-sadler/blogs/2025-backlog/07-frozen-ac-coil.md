## Signs Your AC Coil Is Frozen

A frozen coil usually shows itself in a few ways before you ever see the coil itself.

The evaporator coil sits inside a metal cabinet above your furnace or inside the air handler, so you often can't see it without removing a panel. Look for these clues instead:

- **Frost or ice on the larger copper line at the outdoor unit:** this insulated suction line should feel cold and may sweat in summer, but it should never be white with frost.
- **Weak airflow from the vents:** ice on the coil blocks air from passing through it, so the blower pushes less air into the house even though it sounds normal.
- **Air that's cool but not cold:** the system runs longer and longer while the house stays warm, because a coil covered in ice can't absorb heat properly.
- **Water around the furnace or air handler:** when the ice melts between cycles, the drain pan can overflow and leave a puddle on the floor.

If you're seeing warm air with none of these signs, our guide to [why your AC is not blowing cold air](https://www.johnsadler.ca/blog/ac-not-blowing-cold-air/) covers the other common causes.

## How to Fix a Frozen AC Coil: Step by Step

Thawing the coil is something you can safely do yourself, and it protects the most expensive part of the system while you sort out the cause.

[IMAGE: Thermostat display set to Off with the fan set to On | alt: Thermostat with cooling off and fan on to thaw a frozen AC coil | file: thermostat-fan-on-thaw-frozen-ac.jpg]

1. **Turn cooling off at the thermostat.** Running the AC on a frozen coil can send liquid refrigerant back to the compressor, which can damage it. Switch the system mode to Off, but leave the power on at the breaker so the blower can still run.
2. **Set the fan to On.** Running the blower on its own pulls warm room air across the coil and melts the ice much faster than waiting. In most homes this takes a few hours, though a coil packed with heavy ice can take most of a day.
3. **Protect the floor.** As the ice melts, a lot of water can drain off the coil at once. Put towels around the base of the furnace or air handler and check that the condensate drain line isn't blocked, since a clogged drain can overflow onto the floor.
4. **Check the air filter.** Pull the filter out and hold it up to a light. If you can't see light through it, replace it, since a clogged filter is the most common reason a coil freezes. Our post on [choosing and changing your furnace filter](https://www.johnsadler.ca/blog/furnace-filter/) covers sizes and ratings, because the same filter serves your AC in summer.
5. **Open the vents and clear the returns.** Make sure supply vents in every room are open and that return grilles aren't covered by furniture or rugs. Closing too many vents reduces airflow across the coil and can trigger the same freeze again.
6. **Restart cooling once the ice is gone.** When the copper line is free of frost and the airflow from the vents feels strong again, switch the thermostat back to Cool with the fan on Auto. Check the line again after an hour or two of normal running.

Never chip at the ice, pour hot water on the coil or use a hair dryer or heat gun. The aluminium fins and copper tubing are thin, and a damaged coil turns a simple thaw into a coil replacement.

## Why AC Coils Freeze in the First Place

An AC coil freezes when it gets colder than 0°C, which happens when too little warm air reaches it or when the refrigerant inside is running colder than it should.

### Restricted Airflow

Airflow problems cause most freeze-ups. A dirty filter, closed vents, blocked return grilles or a coil coated in dust all reduce the warm air passing over the coil, so it keeps getting colder until the moisture on it turns to ice. These are the causes you can usually fix yourself.

### Low Refrigerant From a Leak

When the refrigerant charge drops, pressure in the coil drops too, and the coil runs cold enough to freeze. Air conditioners don't use up refrigerant, so a low charge always means a leak somewhere. A hissing sound near the indoor or outdoor unit, or a coil that refreezes within a day or two of thawing with a clean filter, are common signs.

### A Weak Blower Motor or Fan Problem

If the indoor blower is running slowly, or a fan setting was changed during a repair, the system moves less air than it was designed for. Our technicians check blower speed, motor condition and the capacitor that helps start the motor.

### Running the AC on Cool Nights

Running cooling on a cool evening, or setting the thermostat very low for long stretches, can let the coil drop below freezing. On a cool Lower Mainland night, raising the setpoint a few degrees or opening a window is often enough.

## When a Frozen Coil Needs a Licensed Technician

If the coil refreezes after you've replaced the filter and opened the vents, the cause is inside the sealed system or the blower, and it's time for a professional diagnosis.

Refrigerant work in particular is not a DIY repair. In British Columbia, only an approved person may service air conditioning or refrigeration equipment under the provincial [Ozone Depleting Substances and Other Halocarbons Regulation](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/387_99). Topping up refrigerant without finding and repairing the leak only delays the next freeze-up, so our technicians locate the leak first and then explain your repair options.

Also call us if water keeps leaking after the ice has melted, since a cracked drain pan or blocked condensate line can damage floors and ceilings, or if the outdoor unit hums but won't start, which points to a failing capacitor or compressor.

## How to Keep Your AC Coil From Freezing Again

Most freeze-ups can be prevented with a few simple habits and a yearly service.

- **Check the filter monthly in cooling season:** homes with pets, recent renovations or heavy pollen in spring clog filters faster, so a quick monthly look catches problems early.
- **Keep vents and returns open:** closing vents in unused rooms seems like a way to save energy, but it reduces airflow across the coil and can lead to icing.
- **Keep the outdoor unit clear:** trim shrubs back and clear cottonwood fluff and leaves so the unit can release heat properly and the system pressures stay balanced.
- **Book a yearly tune-up before summer:** a technician can clean the coil, check the refrigerant charge, test the blower and clear the condensate drain before the first hot spell.

## Heat Pump Frozen in Winter? That's Often Normal

Frost on a heat pump's outdoor unit in winter is usually not the same problem as a frozen AC coil in summer.

[IMAGE: Heat pump outdoor unit with light frost on its coil on a cold morning | alt: Light frost on a heat pump outdoor unit during a cold Surrey morning | file: heat-pump-frost-winter-surrey.jpg]

In heating mode, the outdoor coil of a heat pump runs colder than the outside air, so on damp days near freezing, frost forms on it. According to [Natural Resources Canada](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/energy-efficiency/energy-star-canada/about/energy-star-announcements/publications/heating-and-cooling-heat-pump/6817), heat pumps handle this with a defrost cycle that briefly switches the unit into cooling mode to send hot refrigerant to the outdoor coil and melt the frost. During defrost you may see steam rising from the unit and notice the outdoor fan stop for a few minutes, which is normal.

It's not normal if the whole outdoor unit is encased in thick ice, if frost stays on the coil for hours, or if ice is building up from water dripping off the roof onto the unit. In those cases, switch to emergency or auxiliary heat if your system has it and book a service call. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does it take a frozen AC coil to thaw?

Most frozen AC coils thaw in a few hours with cooling turned off and the fan set to On. A coil with heavy ice can take most of a day. Wait until the copper line at the outdoor unit is free of frost and airflow from the vents feels strong before you turn cooling back on.

### Can I run my AC while the coil is frozen?

No. Running cooling on a frozen coil can send liquid refrigerant back to the compressor and damage it, and the ice blocks airflow so the house won't cool anyway. Turn cooling off and run the fan on its own until the ice has melted.

### Why does my AC keep freezing up?

The most common cause is restricted airflow from a dirty filter, closed vents or blocked return grilles. If the coil keeps freezing with a clean filter and open vents, the likely causes are low refrigerant from a leak or a weak blower motor. Both need a licensed technician to diagnose and repair.

### Is it normal for a heat pump to freeze in winter?

Light frost on the outdoor unit in winter is normal, and the heat pump removes it with a defrost cycle that runs for a few minutes at a time. Thick ice that covers the whole unit or stays for hours is not normal. That points to a defrost control, sensor or drainage problem that a technician should check.
