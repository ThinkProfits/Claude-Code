# Hot Water Tank Problems and What to Do About Them

> **Quick Answer (Preformatted block):** Most hot water tank problems come down to a pilot or gas supply issue, a tripped breaker or failed element, sediment in the tank, a worn anode rod, or a leaking valve or fitting. You can safely check the pilot, the breaker and where a leak is coming from, but repairs to gas parts, wiring or the tank itself belong with a licensed technician. If you ever smell gas, leave the home and call FortisBC or 911 from outside.

## Safety First

Some situations call for leaving the house, not troubleshooting.

- **You smell gas (a rotten egg or sulphur odour in the air):** avoid light switches, phones and anything that could spark, and go outside. FortisBC asks customers to call its 24-hour emergency line at 1-800-663-9911 or dial 911 once outside, as outlined on its [gas leaks and odour page](https://www.fortisbc.com/safety-outages/energy-safety/natural-gas-safety/gas-leaks-and-odour).
- **Your carbon monoxide alarm goes off:** get everyone outside and call 911. A gas tank that isn't venting properly can push combustion gases back into the home.
- **Water is pooling around an electric tank:** switch off the tank's breaker before you step into the water or touch the tank, since water and live wiring are a dangerous mix.

The checks below are ones a homeowner can do safely. Work on gas valves, burners, thermocouples, venting or electrical parts is a job for a licensed gas fitter or technician.

## No Hot Water, or Not Enough of It

No hot water usually points to the heat source, while hot water that runs out quickly points to something inside the tank. First, check whether every tap is affected. If only one faucet or shower runs cold, the problem is likely that fixture, not the tank.

### 1. No Hot Water From a Gas Tank

Look through the small viewing window near the bottom of the tank for a steady blue pilot flame. If it's out and there's no gas smell, you can follow the lighting instructions printed on the tank's label. Also confirm the control knob is set to "On" and that your other gas appliances are working. If none of them are, the issue may be your gas supply rather than the tank.

### 2. No Hot Water From an Electric Tank

Start at the electrical panel, since a tripped breaker is the simplest explanation and resetting it once is reasonable. If it trips again, or the water stays cold with the breaker on, the likely cause is a failed heating element, thermostat or high-limit switch. Those parts sit behind panels with live wiring, so leave them to a technician.

### 3. Hot Water Runs Out Fast

Sediment is a common culprit here, because minerals settle at the bottom of the tank and make it harder to heat the water. On electric tanks, a failed lower element can cut your usable hot water roughly in half. A broken dip tube (the pipe that carries cold water to the bottom of the tank) can also mix cold water into the hot supply. If the delay is at one particular shower, our article on [why your shower is taking a long time to heat up](https://www.johnsadler.ca/blog/shower-taking-a-long-time-to-heat/) covers the plumbing side.

### 4. The Pilot Light Keeps Going Out

A pilot that lights but won't stay lit usually points to a worn or misaligned thermocouple, the sensor that shuts off the gas when it doesn't detect a flame. A dirty pilot assembly, a draft or a venting problem can also cause it. Many newer tanks have an air intake screen near the base that clogs with lint, so keep the area clear and never store paint or gasoline nearby. If the pilot goes out again after relighting, book a service call rather than relighting it over and over, since a venting issue needs a licensed gas fitter.

## Hot Water Tank Leaking: Find the Source First

Where the water comes from tells you how serious a leak is. Turn off the cold water supply valve on the pipe at the top of the tank, then set a gas tank's control to "Pilot" or "Off," or switch off the breaker for an electric tank. Dry the area with a towel and watch where the water reappears.

### 1. Pipes and Fittings at the Top

Drips from the connections at the top are often a loose fitting or corroded connector, which is usually repairable.

### 2. The Relief Valve or Its Discharge Pipe

The temperature and pressure relief valve opens to release water when pressure or temperature in the tank gets too high. A valve that drips regularly often points to thermal expansion, and we explain the fix, including why we often recommend a potable expansion tank, in our post on [what to do when your thermal relief valve keeps dripping](https://www.johnsadler.ca/blog/thermal-relief-valve-dripping/). Never cap or plug a relief valve yourself.

### 3. The Drain Valve at the Bottom

Drain valves, especially plastic ones, can loosen or wear out over time. If the drip comes from the valve's spout, a technician can usually replace it or fit a cap.

### 4. The Tank Body Itself

Water seeping from the bottom seam or through the outer shell means the inner tank has corroded through, and that can't be repaired. Keep the water supply off and read our guide to [replacing a hot water tank](https://www.johnsadler.ca/blog/replace-water-heater/), which also covers whether a [tankless water heater](https://www.johnsadler.ca/services/plumbing/tankless-water-heaters/) suits your home.

## Noises, Smells and Discoloured Water

These are rarely emergencies, but each one points to something inside the tank.

### 1. Rumbling, Popping or Crackling

A hot water tank making noise is usually a sign of sediment. Water trapped under the mineral layer boils and pops as the burner or element heats it. Most manufacturers recommend flushing the tank at least once a year, and a technician can flush it and check the drain valve at the same visit.

### 2. Rotten Egg Smell in the Hot Water Only

When the smell is in the water itself and only on the hot side, the likely cause is a reaction between bacteria in the water and the tank's anode rod (a metal rod that corrodes in place of the tank lining). A technician can flush the tank and swap the anode rod for a different type, which usually solves it. A gas smell in the air around the tank is a different matter, so follow the safety steps above.

### 3. Rusty or Discoloured Hot Water

Run a cold tap and a hot tap side by side. If both are discoloured, the cause is likely your water supply or pipes, not the tank. If only the hot water is rusty, the anode rod may be used up or the tank may be corroding inside. A worn anode rod can be replaced, but internal rust in the tank itself means replacement is the long-term answer.

## Water That's Too Hot

Scalding water at the tap is a safety issue, especially for young children and older adults. The usual cause is a thermostat set too high or one that has failed and isn't shutting the burner or element off. The Public Health Agency of Canada recommends keeping the water heater at 60°C (140°F) to prevent bacteria such as Legionella, with anti-scald mixing valves set to deliver 49°C (120°F) at the tap, according to its [guidance on water temperature and scalds](https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/water-temperature-burns-scalds.html). If adjusting the setting doesn't change the temperature, have the thermostat checked.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I still use my hot water tank if it's leaking?

It's safest to stop using it until you know where the leak is coming from. Turn off the cold water supply valve at the top of the tank, then set a gas tank to "Pilot" or switch off the breaker for an electric tank. A drip from a fitting or drain valve is usually repairable, but water seeping from the tank body means the tank has failed.

### How often should a hot water tank be flushed?

Most manufacturers recommend flushing a tank at least once a year to clear sediment. Homes that hear rumbling or see sediment in the water may benefit from flushing more often. Check the owner's manual, since some models have specific instructions.

### Is it safe to relight a hot water tank pilot light myself?

On a tank with a standing pilot, it's generally fine to follow the lighting instructions on the tank's label if there is no gas smell. If you smell gas, don't relight it; leave the home and call the gas utility's emergency line or 911 from outside. If the pilot goes out more than once, have a licensed gas fitter find the cause.

### Why does only my hot water smell like rotten eggs?

A smell on the hot side only usually comes from bacteria in the water reacting with the tank's anode rod, which produces a sulphur odour. Flushing the tank and replacing the anode rod with a different type typically clears it up. If both hot and cold water smell, the cause is more likely the water supply.
